How I work

Business-first. Architecture-disciplined.

Every engagement starts with the business objective — what you need to achieve — not with a tool selection or a technology brief.

From there I map capabilities: what your business needs to be able to do. Then architecture: how the systems, data, and processes need to connect to deliver those capabilities. Tools come last — chosen because they fit the architecture, not the other way around.

I use TOGAF as the method — tailored to the engagement. The full ADM has thirteen phases. What always applies: architecture vision before any build, documented principles, a requirements register, and every change recorded with before / after / why / rollback.

Baseline is always separated from target. What is live today is never confused with what is on the roadmap.

Clear scope. Accountable delivery.

Every engagement defines responsibilities, deliverables, and success criteria upfront. I recommend technology investments based on business priorities, existing capabilities, and measurable value.

01 — DISCOVER
Roles first — who owns product, legal, inbox, publish.
02 — BASELINE
Map what is live today — BDAT (Business, Data, Application, Technology). No assumptions.
03 — DEFINE CAPABILITIES AND TARGET STATE
Business needs to be able to do X. Architecture needs to look like Y. Tools follow. Roadmap separated from live.
04 — IMPLEMENT IN SLICES
Thin vertical slices with objective acceptance criteria — not vague phase gates.
05 — RECORD EVERY CHANGE
Before / after / why / rollback — every production change has a change ID.
06 — MEASURE HONESTLY
What is measured today vs what is target — clearly labelled. No vanity metrics.

THREE QUESTIONS WE ANSWER BEFORE STARTING

Who owns what — and who decides?
Product truth, CRM records, website publish, inbox triage — roles and decision rights before tools. Without this, every team member guesses. With it, the desk runs without the founder in every conversation.
What is live today versus what is on the roadmap?
"We have GA4" and "GA4 is measuring what matters" are different things. Baseline is what is live and verified today. Target is the roadmap. Mixing them produces false confidence and wasted spend.
What does success actually look like in 90 days?
Before we touch any tool, we agree what the business needs to be able to do — and what good looks like when we get there. That becomes the acceptance criteria for every piece of work.
